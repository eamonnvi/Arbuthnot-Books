Parallax is the apparent shift in the position of an object when it is viewed from different points; by extension, it describes the way events change their meaning according to the observer’s position. Alan J. Pakula’s 1974 film _The Parallax View_ turns the idea into a political metaphor: apparent facts yield to alternative explanations, identities become unstable, and the attempt to discover a single truth reveals only further perspectives. That idea carries into _Double Exposure_, the third novel in the _Thieves of Time_ sequence. Its movement between Berlin, London and Cambridge repeatedly presents the same people, documents and events from different positions: intelligence files, photographs, microfilm, memories and personal loyalties all disclose something while concealing something else. Truth lies not in finding a privileged point of view, but in noticing the discrepancies between competing ones.
