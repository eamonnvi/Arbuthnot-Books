UNDISCOVERED COUNTRY is a luminous, haunting novel set in the blistering heat of postwar Venezuela. Christina Koning conjures a dazzling, dangerous landscape—oil flames, palm‑shadowed haciendas, shimmering lakes—where expatriate families drift between desire and duty. Through the alert, often wry child’s‑eye of Antonia (Tony) and the fragile, displaced Karel, the story traces exile, memory, and the slow unravel of love: a marriage strained, a community’s secrets, a boy’s inherited terror. Koning’s prose is richly atmospheric—cinematic, tactile, dreamlike—folding in folklore, seaside parties, factory sheds and a shocking night of blood and ritual that will not leave you. Full of tenderness, social unease and quiet menace, UNDISCOVERED COUNTRY is about how the past returns in small acts of cruelty and mercy, how belonging is always provisional, and how children learn what adults prefer to hide. Who survives when a paradise of black gold turns to ash—and who pays?
