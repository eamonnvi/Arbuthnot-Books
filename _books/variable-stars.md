In eighteenth-century Europe, the heavens are changing—and so are the lives of those who study them.

Carolina Herschel arrives in England with little English, little independence and no expectation of greatness. Under the guidance of her brilliant brother William, she becomes a singer, an astronomer and an indispensable observer, searching the night sky for comets and unknown worlds.

In York, the deaf young mathematician John Goodricke finds a language beyond speech in the stars. His friendship with Edward Pigott draws him into a pioneering investigation of variable stars—celestial bodies that brighten, fade and return according to patterns not yet understood. Around them, loyalties shift, families exert their claims, and love proves as elusive and changeable as the heavens.

Rich in period detail and alive with candlelit observatories, frost-bound gardens, music, letters and scientific wonder, Variable Stars is a quietly powerful historical novel about ambition, sacrifice, desire and the women and men whose discoveries challenged a fixed universe.

For readers who enjoy intelligent, atmospheric historical fiction and deeply drawn characters, this is a story of lives illuminated by knowledge—and eclipsed by what can never be possessed.
