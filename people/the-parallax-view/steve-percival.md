### Established identity and circumstances

Steve is a Cambridge-educated writer who studied French and German at St Radegund’s. He is the son of Mavis Percival and has inherited her flat at Trinity Church Square and some money following her death. He works as an assistant sub-editor at Buzz under a false educational story: he claims to have attended the London College of Printing, although he actually read Modern and Medieval Languages at Cambridge (“Buzz - Soho - Monday”; “Xpress Photoset - Clerkenwell - Tuesday”).

His operational name in Berlin was **Tom**. His birth name is explicitly confirmed as **Steve Percival**, although Ulrike initially identifies him through information supplied by Müller and tests the boundaries between his public, personal and operational identities.

### Desires, pressures and loyalties

Steve wants an ordinary, creative life: writing, companionship, literary work and satisfying employment. He also wants emotional and sexual attachment, but repeatedly finds himself drawn into competing obligations involving Grace, Ginny, Sally, Ulrike, Katrin, Sheena and the intelligence service.

His chief pressures are:

- grief over Mavis;
- guilt and uncertainty about the East Berlin operation;
- dependence on Sheena and the department;
- fear that he is being manipulated;
- desire to protect Ulrike and Katrin;
- attraction to Cambridge and to his previous life there;
- uncertainty about whether he is a writer, an operative, or both.

His loyalties shift but do not become simple. He increasingly distrusts the service while continuing to perform tasks that protect its operations. He refuses to expose Collingwood prematurely, partly because he lacks conclusive proof and partly because Sheena directs him to preserve the mole’s usefulness.

### Relationships

- **Mavis:** His mother was his central early attachment. Her death initiates his involvement in espionage. He learns retrospectively that she was an MI6 officer and Müller’s handler.
- **Sheena:** She assumes a guardian-like relationship, combining care, erotic provocation, coercion and professional authority. Steve recognises that she has “done a number” on him but ultimately accepts her appointment as his future handler.
- **Grace:** Their relationship moves from teacher/student and lovers, through coercive separation, estrangement and mutual suspicion, toward renewed sexual and emotional intimacy. Grace’s confession about her Stasi past allows a partial rebalancing, but does not erase the damage.
- **Ginny:** Their connection remains emotionally intense and sexually significant. Ginny claims Steve is Amelia’s biological father, but Steve doubts her account and the manuscript does not independently confirm it.
- **Sally:** Their workplace intimacy develops into a sexual relationship, then becomes complicated by Steve’s operational obligations. Sally ends up with Jeremy but remains emotionally interested in Steve and may reconsider that relationship.
- **Ulrike:** Steve remembers an intense East Berlin encounter and later helps her in London and Cambridge. Much of what he believes about having been with Ulrike is retrospectively destabilised.
- **Katrin:** The manuscript ultimately establishes that Katrin, not Ulrike, was the woman Steve encountered in Prenzlauer Berg and during the first two London meetings. His later love for Katrin becomes the emotional centre of the final section.
- **Müller:** Steve initially dislikes Müller, then develops sympathy and trust. Müller’s archive and dying disclosures transform Steve’s understanding of his own history.
- **Collingwood:** Their relationship is formally that of handler and subordinate, but increasingly contains suspicion and tactical concealment.
- **Doyle and Stan:** Both are older St Rad’s figures who offer forms of mentorship and protection. Steve trusts Stan instinctively and is more guarded with Doyle.

### Agency

Steve repeatedly makes consequential choices despite describing himself as a “plaything”:

- accepts Sheena’s assignment to resume contact with Grace and Müller and monitor Collingwood (“Sheena - Kennington - Tuesday”);
- agrees to assist the woman presented as Ulrike;
- gives Ulrike his address and later shelters her;
- contacts Grace without informing Collingwood;
- chooses not to expose his full suspicions about Collingwood;
- searches Ulrike’s case, which produces evidence but also violates her privacy;
- examines Müller’s Zettelkasten and identifies the separate strands of archive, staging and identity;
- confronts Sheena directly;
- improvises the Paddington Green interview in a way that allows communication with the woman before him;
- accepts the truth of the Paddington-to-Grange substitution;
- participates in Katrin’s staged suicide operation;
- agrees to become Katrin’s future handler.

His agency is often reactive and compromised, but it is not absent. He increasingly learns to preserve uncertainty rather than force premature conclusions.

### Voice and distinguishing features

Steve’s voice is self-deprecating, literary and comic. He uses cultural references to manage embarrassment and danger. He is verbally agile but operationally inexperienced. He is sexually candid yet emotionally cautious. His recurrent awareness of “performance,” “scripts,” “roles” and “stages” makes him a natural participant in the novel’s parallax structure.

### Development

Steve develops from a hesitant, grieving and manipulable young man into someone capable of:

- distrusting institutional explanations;
- reading staged evidence;
- distinguishing proof from interpretation;
- improvising under surveillance;
- accepting that no final explanation may be available;
- taking professional responsibility for an intelligence career he did not initially choose.

He does not become fully confident or morally uncomplicated. His development is toward greater tolerance of ambiguity rather than mastery.

### Knowledge, suspicions and misunderstandings

At the end he knows or has been told that:

- Müller really died of a heart attack;
- the poisoning evidence against Ulrike was manufactured or exploited;
- the apparent suicide at The Grange was staged;
- Katrin is the Stasi twin and Ulrike is the scholar;
- Katrin has been turned by Sheena and returned to East Berlin;
- Collingwood is Tollkirsche and remains in place for operational reasons;
- Sheena arranged the original alternative exfiltration route and kept Steve and Collingwood uninformed;
- his future posting is West Berlin.

He still does not know:

- the full nature of Katrin’s mission;
- whether Collingwood personally caused Mavis’s death;
- the complete provenance of all Zettelkasten additions;
- whether Ginny’s paternity claim is true;
- whether Müller was biologically related to him.

### Position at the end

Steve has resigned from Buzz, accepted MI6 training and been assigned to become Katrin’s handler in West Berlin. His relationship with Grace remains open and “fluid,” while his bond with Katrin is emotionally decisive but physically interrupted. He has gained professional status but not freedom from manipulation..
