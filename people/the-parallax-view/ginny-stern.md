### Established identity and circumstances

Ginny is Steve’s former lover and a visual artist. She is married to Peter Newman and lives in a large inherited house. She has a daughter, Amelia.

Ginny states that Steve is Amelia’s biological father because Peter had undergone a vasectomy. Peter allegedly knows this. Steve remains doubtful.

### Desires, pressures and conflicts

Ginny wants emotional and sexual connection with Steve while retaining her marriage, wealth and social position. She also wants to control the timing and terms of revelation. Her language is enigmatic and often functions as an attempt to bind Steve emotionally.

### Relationships

- **Steve:** Former lovers with continuing sexual and emotional intimacy.
- **Peter:** Husband; their marriage is presented as materially stable but sexually and emotionally limited.
- **Amelia:** Daughter, whose paternity is deliberately concealed and contested.
- **Jude:** Ginny reports that Jude is involved sexually with Peter.
- **Grace:** No direct relationship is shown, but Ginny’s pregnancy affected Grace and Steve’s relationship.

### Agency

Ginny:

- summons Steve to meet Amelia;
- seduces him in the studio;
- reveals the alleged paternity;
- keeps him overnight and pressures him to accept the disclosure;
- offers to involve him more closely in Amelia’s life.

### Voice and behaviour

Ginny is playful, perceptive and mysterious. She often speaks in aphorisms rather than explanations. Her confidence contrasts with Steve’s uncertainty.

### Position at the end

Her relationship with Steve remains emotionally active but structurally impossible. The truth of her paternity claim is unresolved. Neither Peter’s vasectomy nor Steve’s biological fatherhood is independently confirmed.
