### Established identity and circumstances

Peter is Grace’s husband and Ginny’s new husband, chief executive of Inflexion Books and previously associated with the Slade. He is financially successful but faces cash-flow problems. He has had a vasectomy, which he concealed from both Grace and Ginny.

He publicly accepts Ginny’s child as his own despite knowing that Steve is the biological father. His acceptance is partly motivated by access to the Stern trust and partly by his desire to regularise his relationship with Ginny.

### Desires and conflicts

Peter wants control: over Inflexion, his marriage, his public reputation and his financial position. He does not want to become a father in principle, but accepts the situation because Ginny’s pregnancy unlocks Stern money and supports his relationship with her.

His conduct is opportunistic and often coercive, although the narrative also shows genuine insecurity and a desire not to be dependent on Ginny’s wealth.

### Relationships and agency

Peter removes property from the Glisson Road house beyond what Grace considers fair, exploiting the lack of a formal inventory. He proposes to transfer his share in the house to Grace in exchange for a quick divorce. Grace suspects that the offer has an undisclosed financial purpose, probably connected with marrying Ginny and accessing Stern wealth.

He later attends Steve’s first night with Ginny and uses the encounter to display his new marriage and status. His relationship with Ginny is presented as a strategic partnership in which she often has the sharper understanding of the situation.

### Position at the end

Peter is married to Ginny, publicly the father of her child, and expanding Inflexion through acquisitions including Vanguard Books. The biological truth remains private. His divorce from Grace is complete, and he no longer owns the Glisson Road house.
