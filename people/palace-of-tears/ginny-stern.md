### Established identity and circumstances

Ginny is a former catwalk and glamour model, an artist and life model, and Godfrey Stern’s daughter. She has previously had a chaotic personal life, including drug use and depression, but the text also establishes her artistic ability, domestic competence and capacity to improve Steve’s habits.

In the opening scene, she receives a positive pregnancy test. The private position is explicit: Ginny and Peter both know that Peter is not the biological father and understand Steve to be the father. They deliberately present Peter as the father to Godfrey and to the public. Godfrey accepts an ultrasound from one of his own clinics as evidence sufficient for his purposes, but this does not independently establish biological paternity.

Ginny later tells Jude directly that Steve is the father, while Peter knows. This disclosure is strategic and gives Jude power over the situation.

### Desires and pressures

Ginny wants access to her family’s wealth without surrendering autonomy. She wants to re-enter fashionable public life as Virginia Stern, to protect her position with Peter, and to retain emotional or imaginative influence over Steve. Her pregnancy serves several simultaneous purposes: it secures access to the Stern trust, supports Peter’s public respectability and creates a binding future relationship.

The text does not establish whether Ginny consciously planned the pregnancy from the beginning. It does establish that she and Peter knowingly maintain the deception.

### Relationships

With Steve, Ginny is affectionate, manipulative, artistically productive and emotionally evasive. She encouraged his writing and domestic skills, made him a sketchbook and later offered him images of herself. Their encounter in the Great Eastern Hotel demonstrates that she still has sexual and psychological influence over him.

With Peter, she is the more strategically powerful partner once her wealth becomes central. Peter is attracted to her beauty and wealth but fears that she has “worked a number” on him.

With Grace, Ginny is an absent rival whose pregnancy causes Grace considerable pain. Ginny appears to understand this and sometimes deliberately intensifies it.

### Agency

Ginny:

- leaves Steve for Peter;
- persuades Peter to accept the pregnancy;
- uses the Stern trust to stabilise Peter’s company;
- rebrands herself as Virginia;
- writes to Steve;
- arranges the hotel encounter;
- tells Jude that Steve is the biological father;
- encourages Jude to consider a relationship with Steve.

She is not simply a femme fatale. She is also an artist who returns to drawing, a daughter who nurses Godfrey and a woman whose physical and mental health is not always secure.

### Voice and distinguishing features

Ginny’s speech is playful, enigmatic and often apparently prophetic. She claims powers that distort causality, but the text does not establish supernatural ability. Steve interprets her in magical terms because he cannot otherwise explain her effect on him.

Her nudity is connected to professional modelling, bodily confidence and strategic performance. It should not be reduced to mere sexual manipulation, though the narrative explicitly shows her using embodiment to alter power relations.

### Position at the end

Ginny is married to Peter, visibly pregnant and publicly established as the mother of his child. Privately, she, Peter and Jude know that Steve is the biological father, while Grace does not. Ginny’s future influence over Steve and Jude remains open.
