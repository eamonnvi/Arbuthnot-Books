John Keay is a writer and former *Economist* correspondent who lives with Julia and their children at Succoth near Dalmally. He has published *Into India* and *When Men and Mountains Meet* and is completing *The Gilgit Game* when the narrator arrives (**Getting Out of It**).

He is also a practical landowner and smallholder, maintaining livestock, cutting firewood, managing a generator, and undertaking physical work. John’s central commitment is sustained writing combined with an active rural life. He works in a disciplined routine and appears to have constructed the life the narrator envies: family, physical work, exercise, a rural home, and intellectual production.

His practical pressures include weather, fuel, livestock, the financial demands of rural living, and the need to complete his book. The pig-slaughter sequence reveals a tension between care for animals and their intended use as food, though this is primarily the narrator’s conflict.

John is courteous, self-contained, physically capable, and methodical. He does not mock the narrator’s amateur writing and treats the proof-reading offer seriously. His character is distinguished through competence rather than flamboyant speech.

John remains stable across the Dalmally section. The relationship deepens from landlord and tenant to intellectual and practical friendship. At the end of the section, the narrator leaves Succoth with a completed play and an enduring respect for John and Julia.
