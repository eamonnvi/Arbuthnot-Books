### Established identity and circumstances

Ginny says that she grew up in Norfolk, attended a girls’ boarding school, studied at Hornsey College of Art, became a model, and later left the fashion industry after coercive sexual experiences and drug use (“Hot Club de Cambridge”). Grace reports that Ginny’s father survived a concentration camp and that Ginny’s mother died when Ginny was a teenager; Ginny later gives a related but more elaborated account (“Nursing Dr Stern”).

Ginny is an accomplished draughtswoman. Her portfolios contain nudes, portraits, still lifes, landscapes, watercolours and prints. She resumes life drawing and begins to produce self-portraits and studies of Steve.

### Evidentiary caution

Much of Ginny’s biography is self-reported. Jon directly contradicts her account of their relationship, saying that she helped him recover from drug problems and that their separation predated Steve’s arrival. Ginny’s claim that Jon was sexually non-responsive or gay is also contradicted by Jon’s later account, though Jon’s own sexual boasting may be performative. The narrative establishes that Ginny and Peter have an affair and leave together; it does not establish that every element of either character’s backstory is true.

### Desires, pressures and conflicts

Ginny wants artistic recognition, emotional intensity and control over the conditions of intimacy. She is drawn to Steve’s vulnerability and treats him as a person who can be shaped or “helped.” She also seems to want a partner who will accept her complexity without demanding a conventional account of her life.

Her pressure comes from her father’s illness, unresolved family trauma as she describes it, financial dependence or concealment, and her unstable relationship with Jon. She also appears to resist being reduced to a muse, model or damaged survivor.

### Agency

Ginny:
- persuades Steve to accept the Ainsworth Street arrangement;
- cleans and reorganises the house;
- initiates and directs much of her sexual relationship with Steve;
- guides his LSD experience;
- gives him a portable Olivetti;
- reads and critiques *Event/Horizon*;
- resumes drawing and life modelling;
- influences the staging of Steve’s meeting with Angie;
- may have written the false letter to Rob;
- conceals her relationship with Peter;
- leaves Steve and moves to the Barbican with Peter;
- leaves Steve a sketchbook and £100.

She is the most materially effective character in the domestic sphere. She transforms houses, gardens, meals, routines and artistic situations. At the same time, her ability to manage circumstances often shades into manipulation.

### Voice, behaviour and distinguishing features

Ginny shifts between “ice maiden,” “sibylline,” “domestic goddess,” “odalisque,” “muse,” “model” and “dominatrix” modes. The narrative explicitly notices these shifts, but they may be Steve’s categorisations rather than objective identities. She is often enigmatic, controlled and economical in speech, yet can become practical, nurturing or sharply confrontational.

Her relationship to the gaze is complex. She has been objectified as a model, but also draws, stages, critiques and directs others. Her work complicates Steve’s assumption that nudity necessarily means sexual availability, even though she sometimes deliberately uses erotic ambiguity.

### Relationships and development

- **Jon:** Their relationship is established as intimate but undefined. Both offer incompatible accounts of who rescued or used whom. They remain functionally connected until Jon leaves for the tour.
- **Steve:** Begins as a practical housemate and becomes a sexual and artistic relationship. Ginny encourages him but also frames herself as someone who can “free” or possess him. She eventually abandons the relationship for Peter.
- **Peter:** Their affair develops through the life-drawing group and London/Frankfurt meetings. The extent of prior planning is uncertain, but the relationship is confirmed by their departure together.
- **Grace:** Ginny joins Grace’s life class and appears to respect her artistic ability. Their relationship also contains rivalry, particularly after Peter enters the group.
- **Angie:** Ginny treats Angie as a rival or potential threat, though she insists that she wants to secure Steve’s commitment to her.

### Position at the end of *Event/Horizon*

Ginny has removed herself and her possessions from Ainsworth Street and is living, or preparing to live, with Peter in his Barbican flat. She leaves Steve a sketchbook, money and a deliberately restrained letter. Her exact feelings for Steve are unresolved: she says she cared for him, but her behaviour indicates that she had planned to move on while maintaining the relationship.
